\section{Introduction}

Consumer services increasingly use photographs as evidence: a guest reports a pest in a hotel room, a diner reports contamination in a meal, or a tenant reports damage in a rental. Such images can affect refunds, platform penalties, reputation, and insurance decisions, yet they are captured in uncontrolled settings and often reviewed under time pressure. Modern editing changes the threat. An attacker need not synthesize a whole scene; a small, plausible object inserted into a real photograph can change the meaning of the claim.

This paper studies that mostly-real threat through the current canonical mouse core of CLAIMFORGE. Each forged image is paired with its real source, and the pre-encoding exact RGB differences occupy a median 0.11264\% of the frame. Real and forged inputs receive the same final JPEG encoding. The setting is therefore different from whole-image generation: global statistics are dominated by the source photograph, while a useful review tool should provide both a reliable image-level warning and spatial evidence identifying what changed.

We ask three questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Can current methods detect these tiny edits at low false-positive rates? \textbf{RQ2:} Can they localize the changed evidence accurately enough for review? \textbf{RQ3:} How does the strongest joint model vary with edit size and descriptive domain slices? We answer these questions on 275 human-approved real/forged pairs using eight fully reproduced research methods. We preserve methods' registered capabilities: a localization-only method remains N/A for detection rather than receiving an improvised score.

We make four evidence-backed contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize a consumer-evidence threat and paired tasks for image-level manipulation detection and exact changed-pixel localization.
    \item We construct and audit a canonical mouse core with human quality control, matched container encoding, and exact-difference masks.
    \item We introduce capability-aware reporting that preserves N/A outputs and evaluates low-FPR detection, localization, and pristine-image false positives.
    \item We show a substantive but incomplete signal: TruFor is the strongest joint model, CAT-Net is a strong localization-only model, and no reproduced method clears both study-specific base-condition references on the canonical mouse condition.
\end{itemize}

The conclusion is deliberately scoped. We do not claim cross-editor or laundering robustness, and we do not transfer mouse-core results to the unevaluated cat and trash-can expansions. The current result is a paired stress test of one difficult, operationally relevant condition.
