A small object insertion can change the apparent meaning of a consumer-evidence photograph even when nearly every pixel still comes from a real camera image. We introduce the canonical mouse core of CLAIMFORGE, a paired stress test for small AI object insertions in restaurant and lodging photographs. The core contains 275 human-approved source/forged pairs. Its pre-encoding exact RGB-difference masks cover a median 0.11264\% of the image, after which both sides of every pair are freshly encoded with identical JPEG settings and inherited EXIF stripped. We reproduce eight open-source local-forensics methods under their registered native detection and localization capabilities. TruFor is the strongest joint research method, reaching 0.8179 image AUROC, 0.4436 TPR at 5\% FPR, and 0.5001 macro pixel F1. CAT-Net reaches the highest pixel AP, 0.6123, but has no registered image-level head. On this canonical condition, no reproduced method simultaneously reaches the study-specific base-condition references of 0.90 AUROC and 0.50 macro pixel F1. Performance also varies sharply with edit size: TruFor AUROC rises from 0.7223 in the smallest quintile to 0.9412 in the largest. These results reveal meaningful forensic signal, but not reliable coverage of both capabilities motivated by consumer-claim review.
